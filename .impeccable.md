## Design Context

### Users

BuckyOS App Service serves three overlapping audiences: ordinary users who need to see whether installed apps are healthy and start or stop them; developers and early integrators who install apps directly from URLs, App DID/App Meta content, or `.pikg` files and diagnose failures; and system maintainers who need to distinguish app failures from system-service, kernel, Docker engine, image, or container problems.

The primary jobs are to understand what is running, move directly from an app to its details, install an app through a trustworthy and recoverable task flow, change app settings, and understand the next action when something fails.

### Brand Personality

Calm, direct, and engineering-led. The interface should feel dependable and precise without becoming hostile to non-technical users. It explains source, trust, permissions, progress, and failure in plain language, while preserving deeper technical identifiers for users who need them.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined system-console aesthetic rather than an app-store or marketing aesthetic. Preserve the BuckyOS desktop's Plus Jakarta Sans/Sora typography, semantic `--cp-*` theme tokens, and both light and dark themes. Favor clear hierarchy, compact status rows, restrained surfaces, explicit task stages, and progressive disclosure of technical detail. Avoid promotional imagery, recommendation patterns, decorative gradients, excessive cards, and neon or terminal cosplay.

### Design Principles

1. Put installed apps and their current state first; system services and kernel remain visible but visually quieter.
2. Make trust, permissions, task stage, and failure reasons explicit; parsing success must never imply verification or trust.
3. Keep common actions understandable for ordinary users, with technical identifiers and evidence available through progressive disclosure.
4. Treat installation as one recoverable `task_id` transaction whose progress and result remain distinguishable from app runtime state.
5. Reuse BuckyOS theme tokens and interaction patterns, support keyboard and touch use, and adapt rather than remove critical controls on narrow screens.
