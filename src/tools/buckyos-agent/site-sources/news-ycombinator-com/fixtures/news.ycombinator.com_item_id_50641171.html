Interesting for audio nerds: Windows actually has an audio limiter. If you playback audio via loopback shared WASAPI, DirectSound or MME it actually gets compressed very slightly as it nears the peaks. The same does not happen via WASAPI exlcusive or ASIO.

Playing loudly mastered audio out of your PC will be very slightly different from the true audio source by about -0.1dbps with the compressor affecting the peaks.

It works via audiodg.exe (CAudioLimiter in AudioEng.dlll) on the shared-mode render path after all the streams get mixed, even if there's just one playing currently. This is necessary because two loud signals being mixed together might clip your DAC and that sounds nasty, so it's understandable that it's there.

Why would you care though? I was building a audio metering system to monitor audio via loopback and the properties of the audio I played in were being modified by the limiter in these normal modes. Maybe audio purists might not enjoy it either.
